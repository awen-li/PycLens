
\subsection{RQ1: Bytecode Prevalence and Transparency}\label{sec:effmeasure}

RQ1 measures how often Python bytecode artifacts appear in collected PyPI distributions and whether they are transparent to source-level inspection.  For each package, the collector keeps at most one wheel and one source distribution when available.  This package-balanced design prevents projects with many uploaded files from dominating the measurement, but it also means that wheels and source distributions should be interpreted as separate strata.  We therefore use the wheel and source-distribution rows as the primary prevalence estimates and report the combined row only as a descriptive total over collected artifacts.  We count artifacts containing \texttt{.pyc} files, \texttt{\_\_pycache\_\_} directories, source-less bytecode candidates, and dynamic loading indicators.  Results are reported both per distribution artifact and per bytecode file because one artifact can contain many bytecode files.

The wheel and source-distribution rows answer the prevalence part of RQ1.  In wheels, 2{,}646 of 517{,}076 artifacts contain bytecode (0.51\%).  In source distributions, 4{,}702 of 517{,}077 artifacts contain bytecode (0.91\%).  Thus, bytecode artifacts appear in both distribution forms, with a higher rate in source distributions in our collected dataset.  The transparency part of RQ1 is measured at the bytecode-file level: across collected artifacts, the scanner finds 228{,}578 \texttt{.pyc} files, including 28{,}193 that are source-less under our conservative path-based check.  These results establish that bytecode artifacts are not merely theoretical inputs: they occur in package distributions at a scale that package-security workflows can encounter.  The observed version spread in Table~\ref{tab:bytecode-versions} motivates the version-aware analysis used for RQ2.
